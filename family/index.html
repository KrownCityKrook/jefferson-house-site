Bringing the Kids

Built so you can actually sit down.

A splash pad they'll run straight for, a courtyard you can see across, and no stairs anywhere. Here's what a family afternoon here looks like.

The Splash Pad

Sixteen feet of the main event.

The splash pad sits at the west end of the courtyard — a bubbler-style pad, which means no standing water and no depth to worry about. It was in the plans from the start rather than added as an afterthought, and it's the reason a lot of families will come at all.

Practical advice: bring a towel and a change of clothes. Assume they're getting in, because they are.

Shade & Heat

Outdoors, but out of the sun.

This is Arizona, so it's the first thing worth answering. The entire courtyard sits under a covered black-steel canopy with fans and misters for the summer and heaters for the winter. You're outside the whole time, and you're not in direct sun.

It won't be February-cool in July. But it's a genuinely usable outdoor space in months when most patios in the Valley aren't.

Getting Around

One flat level, no steps.

The courtyard is a single grade with wide paths between the kitchens, shops, bar and stage — enough room for a double stroller to get through on a busy night without a three-point turn. Restrooms are accessible.

Parking is free street and surface-lot parking all around the block, so you park once and unload once.

Food

Nobody has to agree on one restaurant.

Three kitchens and six retail and eatery spots, every one of them independently owned. That's the practical advantage of a courtyard over a restaurant with kids in tow: everyone eats what they want, at the same table, at the same time.

We'll publish who's in the building as tenants are announced — the news page is where that will land.

Parent Questions

The ones we get asked.

Is Jefferson House kid-friendly?

Yes. The courtyard is all ages, the 16-foot splash pad was built for kids, and the whole space is step-free. Some evening events are 21+, and we say so clearly when they are.

Tell me about the splash pad.

It's a 16-foot bubbler-style splash pad in the west end of the courtyard — no standing water and no depth, so it works for little kids. Bring a towel and a change of clothes, because they will use it.

Can I bring a stroller?

Easily. The courtyard is one flat level with no steps and wide paths between the kitchens, shops, bar and stage. Restrooms are accessible.

Is there shade? It's Arizona.

The entire courtyard sits under a covered black-steel canopy with fans and misters for summer and heaters for winter. You're outdoors the whole time, but out of direct sun.

When is it best to bring kids?

Daytime. We open at 9am on Saturday and Sunday and 10am on weekdays, and mornings and afternoons in the courtyard are bright, quiet and easy. Later evenings get busier and louder.

Will there be food kids actually eat?

Three kitchens and six retail and eatery spots, all independently owned, so there will be range. Nobody has to agree on one restaurant, which is the practical advantage of a courtyard.
