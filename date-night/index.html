Date Night

Not two hours across a table.

The best thing about a courtyard on a date is that you can get up and move. Here's the honest case for spending an evening here.

Why It Works

Five reasons, no marketing.

  1. You don't need a reservation Turn up when you turn up. There's no table you're late for and no cover to get in, which takes most of the pressure out of a first date and all of it out of a fifteenth.
  2. You can move Drinks at the bar, food from one kitchen, something sweet from a shop, then over toward the stage. A date here is a walk with stops rather than a single seat for two hours.
  3. The food isn't one decision Three kitchens and six eatery spots means you don't have to negotiate a cuisine before you leave the house. Order separately, share anyway.
  4. There's usually music The stage faces the courtyard, so a band is the room's atmosphere rather than something you're seated in front of. Easy to talk over, or not, as you prefer.
  5. You can leave early or stay late We're open until 1:30am Friday and Saturday. Nothing about the night commits you to a length — and if it's going badly, nobody's bringing you a dessert course.

Practically Speaking

Park once, walk the district.

Free street and surface-lot parking surrounds the block, so neither of you is circling. We're at 83rd Avenue and Jefferson Street in Downtown Peoria's Arts District — the Peoria Center for the Performing Arts is a few minutes' walk, which makes a show-then-drinks evening straightforward.

The courtyard is covered with heaters, fans and misters, so an outdoor date is a reasonable plan in January and in July. See hours and parking for the details.

One Caveat

It's a courtyard, not a quiet room.

If what you want is a hushed corner and a tasting menu, we're honestly not it — and Phoenix has better options for that night. This is an open-air space with kids at the splash pad in the afternoon and a band on the stage later. It's lively by design.

That's the trade. We think it makes for a better evening more often than not, but you should know what you're choosing.
